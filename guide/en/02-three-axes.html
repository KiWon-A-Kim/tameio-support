2. Three Axes to Know First — Accounts, Categories, Budgets

The hardest part of starting with Tameio is not operating the screens; it is telling apart three different concepts and the job each one does. Once you can separate these three axes, the way transactions, budget funding, and recurring rules behave will make sense on its own.

Accounts — where money sits, or where debt accrues

An account is the place money actually is. Bank accounts, savings, investment accounts, cash, and credit cards are all accounts. A credit card is different in that what accumulates there is not money but debt you have to repay — yet it belongs to the same axis, because it still answers "where did this move from?"

  • An account balance is not a number you type in and store. It is always recalculated from the transactions in that account. Enter, edit, or delete a transaction and the balance reflects it immediately.
  • The "starting balance" you enter when creating an account is not a stored number either. It automatically creates one transaction that brings the account to that balance.

Categories — what you spent it on, or where you earned it

A category classifies the nature of a transaction. Food, transport, salary, side income — it answers "what kind of money was this?" If an account is where, a category is what.

Two expenses from the same account (one credit card, say) can have different categories — food for one, transport for the other. Conversely, expenses in the same category (food) can come from several different accounts and cards. The important point is that accounts and categories are independent axes.

Budget groups and allocation — how much you plan to spend

A budget group is a unit that bundles several categories under one spending limit. For example, you can put the Food and Cafe/Snacks categories into a single Living budget group and manage how much they may spend together in a month.

  • Allocating is the act of deciding how much of the money you actually have should be entrusted to which budget group. An allocation is not a transaction and does not change any account balance.
  • Creating a category and connecting it to a budget group are two separate decisions. If you create a category but do not connect it to a budget group, spending in that category does not count against any particular group — it reduces the overall Available to allocate (pool) instead.
